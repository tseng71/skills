# Pudding story pattern library

This pattern library is based on close reading of 11 live Pudding stories and their visible openings, narrative progression, visual states, controls, methods, fallbacks, and endings. Last reviewed 2026-07-28.

Use these references for structural and editorial logic. Do not imitate The Pudding's logo, typography, house styling, illustrations, or exact layouts.

## Dicing an Onion, the Mathematically Optimal Way

[Dicing an Onion, the Mathematically Optimal Way](https://pudding.cool/2025/08/onions/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- opens with a plain-language question attached to a familiar object;
- uses the onion itself as title art, explanatory diagram, and data-generating model;
- lets readers notice visibly inconsistent pieces before defining relative standard deviation;
- introduces vertical cuts, radial cuts, target depth, layers, and horizontal cuts one variable at a time;
- keeps the onion cross-section stable while the cutting rules change;
- uses “explode” as a meaningful view: it exposes piece-size variation rather than decorating the chart;
- moves from authored examples to sliders, then to a table of all 19,320 modeled combinations;
- explicitly distinguishes a finite simplified model from real 3D onions and from the limiting mathematical case;
- ends by asking whether mathematical optimality matters in cooking, returning the technical result to everyday stakes with humor.

Use when a physical object can become a measurable model. Teach the metric only after the reader can see the phenomenon it measures.

## Bears Will Be Boys

[Bears Will Be Boys](https://pudding.cool/2025/07/kids-books/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- opens with the author's own reflexive wording—“Mr. Frog”—before stating the research question;
- combines a manually coded corpus of roughly 300 books with an experiment involving more than 1,300 respondents;
- explains conservative gender coding choices beside concrete book-page examples;
- gives the project a subject-native world: children’s-book colors, animal illustrations, and playful typography;
- uses animal illustrations as the marks on a he/him-to-she/her position scale, unifying illustration and statistics;
- compares book results with survey results using the same ordered animal entities;
- includes respondent text and book excerpts so the distribution never becomes anonymous;
- places the searchable/filterable field of 800+ characters after the guided argument;
- keeps methods, data download, and representation caveats available.

Use when culture, perception, and categorization can be tested with both corpus data and an experiment. Do not let the custom illustration disappear when the statistical chart begins.

## 30 minutes with a stranger

[30 minutes with a stranger](https://pudding.cool/2025/06/hello-stranger/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- begins with one pair who do not know each other;
- makes the 30-minute duration the organizing spine, with explicit second-by-second chronology;
- identifies the pair as part of nearly 1,700 conversations only after establishing the human scene;
- uses pseudonyms and states the privacy reason in the main experience;
- allows readers to select people while the authored timeline continues;
- expands from one pair to demographic and affect comparisons across the corpus;
- synchronizes conversational sequence, people, and mood changes rather than treating media as an illustration beside a chart;
- ends in the author’s ethical and emotional conclusion about safety between strangers, not in a corpus browser.

Use for interviews, conversations, performances, or event sequences. Preserve the concrete pair as the population appears.

## The Pour-igin of Species

[The Pour-igin of Species](https://pudding.cool/2025/04/wine-animals/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- opens before the title with a realistic shopping situation and four actual bottles;
- asks readers to choose, but clearly allows scrolling to skip;
- later pays off the choice with data about price, rating, and value;
- begins with recognizable bottle objects and animal labels before abstracting to category medians;
- assembles a scatterplot progressively: axes, overall medians, groups, zoom-out, individual wines, trend, then quadrants;
- transforms “best” into an explicit reader-relevant definition of a good deal;
- lets readers alter thresholds and filters only after teaching the default definition;
- uses the object lineup again for animal-by-animal browsing;
- discloses automated image classification errors and manual corrections;
- ends with one concrete bottle and the author's own geographic coincidence, returning to the consumer scene.

Use when readers have a natural prior choice. Store that choice for a later reveal, but make the story work for readers who skip.

## The United States of Abortion Mazes

[The United States of Abortion Mazes](https://pudding.cool/2024/10/abortion-mazes/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- begins with the phrase “path to access,” then slowly allows maze walls to occupy the screen;
- turns 28 weighted policy measures into maze complexity, making the visual metaphor carry the analytical claim;
- uses 51 small multiples so state differences are visible as a system rather than isolated anecdotes;
- offers six carefully contextualized personal stories as routes through the policy landscape;
- lets readers pick a story or see all mazes without requiring play;
- provides sorting, jump controls, progress, and a downloadable activity-book form;
- explains how stories were sourced and altered for privacy;
- states that the law is changing and that the project is a time-bounded snapshot;
- exposes the weighting logic and the maze-generation algorithm.

Use when constraints can become rules or paths. On sensitive topics, interaction must create understanding, not entertainment; always include a non-playable path.

## This is a teenager

[This is a teenager](https://pudding.cool/2024/03/teenagers/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- opens with one pseudonymous 13-year-old, Alex, whose circumstances and optimism coexist;
- introduces hundreds of other participants from a longitudinal survey while keeping Alex identifiable;
- uses persistent person silhouettes as the semantic units across 24 years;
- moves the same people through risk groups, education, work, income, health, happiness, and loss;
- places years and average age in a persistent frame so time is never an implicit filter;
- lets readers click a person for details without destroying the population view;
- uses secondary contextual charts only when they explain the institutional mechanism around the cohort;
- returns to Alex at age 37 and reframes adult outcomes as collective responsibility;
- provides a video alternative and an end-state data explorer.

Use for longitudinal records. Keep people as people across states; do not replace them with aggregate bars as soon as the analysis becomes difficult.

## Quantifying the Diva-ness of National Anthem Performances

[Quantifying the Diva-ness of 138 National Anthem Performances](https://pudding.cool/2024/02/anthems/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- begins with familiar contrasting performances before introducing a derived score;
- maps pitch to visible paths and normalizes performances to the same key for comparison;
- zooms from whole-song paths to one of 16 lyrical phrases at a time;
- pairs selectable performer portraits with audio, so the reader can hear what the line shape means;
- repeats one learned visual grammar while varying phrase, event, genre, key, and performer;
- distinguishes melodic deviation from aesthetic quality—the score measures difference, not whether a rendition sounds good;
- ends the guided phrase-by-phrase tour with a heatmap of all performers and phrases;
- provides filters and sorting only after readers know how to listen to and read the data;
- explains trimming, vocal isolation, manual pitch correction, and Dynamic Time Warping in methods.

Use for sound, speech, or performance data. Synchronize the original medium with the analytical marks and state explicitly what the metric cannot judge.

## 24 hours in an invisible epidemic

[24 hours in an invisible epidemic](https://pudding.cool/2023/09/invisible-epidemic/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- opens at 4:30 a.m. with one sleepless 62-year-old, Martin;
- makes the reader move minute by minute through a typical weekend day;
- uses duration and empty time as evidence instead of compressing the day immediately into a summary;
- introduces additional people only after Martin's isolation is legible;
- allows inspection of individual people while maintaining the shared clock;
- converts different kinds of social contact into a cumulative measure and explains the acquaintance weighting;
- inserts research and aggregate context at natural checkpoints in the day;
- repeatedly returns to Martin's mundane actions, making absence visible without melodrama;
- closes at bedtime with the author's personal articulation of loneliness.

Use when waiting, repetition, absence, or accumulation is the claim. Continuous scroll must be punctuated by authored insights; otherwise it becomes ornamental length.

## A Map of Places in the US with the Same Name

[A Map of Places in the US with the Same Name](https://pudding.cool/2023/03/same-name/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- leads with the finding and a search box rather than a long introduction;
- matches the reader's natural question: “Which place does this name usually mean from here?”;
- offers curated names for readers who do not know where to start;
- makes the map the primary explanatory and exploratory object;
- labels the model as opinionated and a conversation starter;
- links to a deeper explainer where assumptions can be customized;
- keeps a visible changelog and invites corrections for a living, imperfect dataset.

Use for lookup-first geospatial questions. A useful default, curated examples, caveats, and an authored explanation are required; a search box alone is not a story.

## Why the super rich are inevitable

[Why the super rich are inevitable](https://pudding.cool/2022/12/yard-sale/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- opens with the author overpaying for a vintage watch;
- turns “surely it evens out” into the reader's prediction;
- teaches the model through two transactions before simulating a population;
- keeps balances and players stable so the reader can follow the asymmetric wager;
- delays the 10,000-round result until the micro-mechanism is understood;
- includes a two-player coin-flip interaction that embodies the feedback loop;
- separates the model's explanatory value from claims that it precisely represents a real economy;
- adds expert context after the core mechanism is visible.

Use for counterintuitive causal systems. Start with a minimal playable rule, then scale it. Never let a simulation's visual authority exceed its assumptions.

## How are you doing?

[How are you doing?](https://pudding.cool/2022/12/emotion-wheel/)

Narrative and visual pattern:

- begins with an everyday question and the author's own unsatisfying answers;
- guides the reader through words, colors, body sensations, and needs;
- uses reader reflection as the central interaction, not as a quiz with a correct answer;
- states sensitive mental-health content and invites readers to take care;
- provides a pause control and an all-text version;
- keeps participation optional and allows activities to open separately;
- ends by returning to “How are you doing?” with a changed vocabulary and more tenderness.

Use for reflective, participatory, or care-centered stories. Build consent, pause, skip, and equivalent-text access into the main design rather than adding them as footer notes.

## Process reference: Can an AI make a data-driven, visual story?

[Can an AI make a data-driven, visual story?](https://pudding.cool/2024/07/ai/)

Useful process lessons:

- generic prompts generate generic pitches and familiar charts;
- idea, data/analysis, storyboarding/prototyping, and development/writing are distinct stages;
- prototyping a plausible interface is easier than finding a story with a defensible point of view;
- a specific example can open into the full dataset;
- repeated editorial and visual iteration separates a competent result from a strong one;
- exposing the process works only because process is the subject of this story.

## Cross-story rules

Across these stories:

- the opening unit is usually smaller than the dataset: one person, object, choice, pair, place, or transaction;
- subject matter supplies the marks and motion whenever possible;
- complex charts are assembled in front of the reader;
- the same entities persist across multiple claims;
- authored explanation precedes unrestricted exploration;
- interaction answers a real reader question and a skip/default path remains;
- methods disclose transformations and limits close enough to affect interpretation;
- visual intensity changes across the story;
- endings return to a human or concrete opening instead of stopping at “explore.”
